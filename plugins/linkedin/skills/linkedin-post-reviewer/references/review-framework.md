# Review framework: 7 dimensions

Score each dimension from 1 to 5:
- **5:** strong, don't touch it.
- **4:** good, one small fix.
- **3:** works, but a clear fix would lift the post.
- **2:** weak, holds the post back.
- **1:** missing or broken.

Not every dimension needs written feedback. Focus on what moves performance most. The hook almost always matters most.

## 1. Hook strength (most important)

If the first 1 to 3 lines don't stop the scroll, nothing else matters.

Check:
- Does it open a value gap, spark curiosity, challenge a belief or make a bold claim?
- Does it fit in 140 characters (the safe zone on every device)?
- Does it lead with the strongest element: a number, a result, a contrarian take?
- Would you stop scrolling for it at 11pm?

Common problems:
- Starting with context instead of the payoff. "I recently had a conversation with a client about..." becomes "This is how a 12-person agency books 30 calls a month:".
- The number or result buried in line 2 or 3.
- A weak teaser opener ("Didn't expect this:") when the real hook sits in the next line ("We stopped cold email. Pipeline went up 40%.").

## 2. Structure and scannability

LinkedIn is read while scrolling, mostly on mobile.

Check:
- Short paragraphs, 1 to 3 lines.
- White space between sections.
- Lists for scannable details, in the user's own list style (arrows, dashes or numbers).
- A clear rhythm: hook, context, details or list, CTA.

Common problems:
- Walls of text.
- Lists longer than 7 or 8 items, which start to feel like a dump.
- No white space between logical sections.

## 3. Specificity and proof

Specific details build trust. Vague claims kill it.

Check:
- Concrete numbers: people, growth, time saved, cost.
- Named tools or methods (named companies or people only when the user has permission).
- Before and after: what took X now takes Y.
- Social proof: attendees, replies, results.

Common problems:
- "So many people" instead of the real number.
- A number without context. "300% growth" is good. "300% growth with a team of 4" is powerful.
- Features listed without outcomes.

## 4. Value clarity

The reader should know within seconds what they get.

Check:
- Is the value clear in the first 3 to 5 lines?
- Is there something the reader can act on?
- Does the body deliver what the hook promised?

Common problems:
- Teasing value that never arrives ("Here's what I learned" then vague lessons).
- Several ideas competing in one post.
- A hook that promises one thing and a body that delivers another.

## 5. Engagement architecture

The post should invite one clear action, in the user's usual CTA style.

Check:
- One clear, simple CTA at the end. Judge it against how the user usually ends posts (comment keyword, question, link in comments, DM, or none).
- It feels like a fair exchange: "I give you value, you engage".
- At least one element invites a reaction: a bold claim, a surprising number, a useful resource.

Common problems:
- A weak or missing CTA, when the post's goal needs one.
- A CTA buried in the middle.
- Asking for too much (comment and share and tag someone).
- A CTA that feels transactional instead of generous.

If the user's goal is reach or saves rather than comments, say how the CTA could serve that goal instead.

## 6. Length and pacing

Guidelines:
- About 150 to 250 words for a standard post, up to 350 for a deep-value post.
- Every line earns its place. If removing it doesn't hurt, remove it.
- The pace speeds up toward the CTA.

Common problems:
- Filler that repeats what was already said.
- Throat-clearing paragraphs that delay the value.
- Lists that could be cut to the strongest 5 or 6 items.

## 7. Authenticity check

The final gut check: does this sound like the user? Compare with their voice skill or the posts they shared.

Check:
- Would they say this out loud? If not, it's too polished.
- Does it feel like insider knowledge shared, or like content marketing?
- Any phrases that sound AI-written? ("In today's fast-paced world", "Let's dive in", "Here's the thing", "unlock", "game-changer")

Red flags:
- Buzzwords (synergy, leverage, unlock, game-changer).
- Passive voice or third person, when the user writes in first person.
- Sentences far more polished than the user's own posts. Their small imperfections are part of how they sound.

## Thinking like the feed

LinkedIn rewards dwell time (people reading to the end), comments (especially real ones) and shares or saves. Feedback should consider what drives those, without pushing tactics that don't fit the user's voice.
