# Ideas, hooks and drafts

## Pulling ideas from the transcript

Look for what only you would say:
- **Things you do differently** from the usual advice in your field.
- **Choices you defend:** "I never do X, because Y."
- **Numbers from your own setup or results:** time saved, costs, conversion, team size.
- **Analogies and everyday comparisons** you used live. They often land better than anything written.
- **Questions attendees asked.** A question three people asked is a post. Use roles, never names.
- **Moments where the energy changed:** a strong reaction, a laugh, a "wait, really?".

Ignore the pitch for your offer, logistics, sound checks and housekeeping.

For each candidate idea, check it against your audience (from your ICP and Messaging pages, or profile file): would this person stop for it?

Flag any number you said loosely so you can check it.

## Hook patterns

When you have data, borrow patterns from your own best posts and name the model post. When you don't, these are common patterns that work on LinkedIn. The examples are invented.

- **Number confession:** "I spent 40 hours on proposals last month. Now it's 6."
- **One word in CAPS against expectations:** "Cold calls are NOT dead. Bad lists are."
- **Calling out the audience:** "Agency owners: stop pricing by the hour."
- **Someone else's quote** (only if someone really said it to you): "\"We don't need marketing, we need clients.\" A founder told me this last week."
- **Result, then setup:** "We booked 22 calls from one webinar. Here is the setup."
- **Experiment framing:** "I tested 3 follow-up emails on 200 leads. One won by a mile."

For more options on one idea, use the `linkedin-hook-generator` skill.

## The 3 draft angles

Write 3 versions with the same hook, each a different angle. For example:
1. **Step by step:** the method or filter from the session, in 3 to 6 clear steps.
2. **Contrarian stance with an everyday analogy:** the opinion you defended, made simple with one comparison from daily life.
3. **The setup, with a soft pointer to the session:** what you use and how, ending with `[link]` to the recording or the next session, if you want one.

Pick other angles if the idea fits them better. Say which one you'd post and why, in one line.

## Writing the drafts

Follow your voice skill, or match the 3 to 5 posts you shared. Then check:
- Short sentences and white space between lines. Don't overdo it: not every sentence needs its own line.
- No em dashes or double dashes, unless your own posts use them.
- One everyday comparison, one rule of thumb, first person.
- 150 to 250 words for a standard post, up to 350 for a deep one.
- Your usual CTA style at the end (comment keyword, question, link in comments, or none).
- No invented facts, results or quotes. Placeholders like `[number]` and `[link]` are flagged.
- No names of prospects, clients or attendees.

## Output formats

**Checkpoint 1 (ideas):**
```
1. [Working hook]
   Angle: ...
   Why: like your post "[hook]" ([metric]) / your audience's pain "[words]".
   Overlap: none / similar to "[past post]".
...
My top 3: #_, #_, #_. Which one?
```

**Checkpoint 2 (hooks):**
```
1. [Hook]  (pattern: result then setup, modelled on "[past post]", [metric])
...
Recommended: #_. Backup: #_.
```

**Checkpoint 4 (final):** the approved post in one plain text block, ready to paste into LinkedIn, followed by one line on what to check before posting.
