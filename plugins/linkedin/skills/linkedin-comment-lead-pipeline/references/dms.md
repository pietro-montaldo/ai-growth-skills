# DM drafts

The DM should read like it took you ten seconds to send: short, friendly, one reason to reply. It points to the offer you confirmed in "Before starting".

## Write one template, in your voice

Use your voice skill, or the DMs and posts you shared. Then write one template with variables. The shape (invented example, rewrite it in your voice):

```
Hey <first name>, saw your comment on <post topic>. I'm running <offer> <when>. Thought it could be useful: <link>
```

For reactors, change the opening to something true, like "saw you engaged with my post on <post topic>". Never say someone commented when they only reacted.

Show the template and 2 filled examples. Wait for your OK before filling it for everyone.

## Filling the variables

- `<first name>`: first name only. Never the full name or a title.
- `<post topic>`: what the post was actually about, in 2 or 3 plain lowercase words. Not the post title, no quotes, no brackets. A post about turning sales calls into follow-up emails becomes: call follow-ups.
- `<offer>`: the offer's plain name ("a free workshop", "a short checklist", "a 20-minute teardown"). Use only what is on its page.
- `<when>`: only if it's true and known ("next week", "on the 9th"). Otherwise drop it and reshape the sentence.
- `<link>`: the offer URL. If you have none yet, leave `[link]` and flag it.

## Personal or the same for everyone?

By default, every DM uses the same template, with only the variables changed. No compliments, no quoting what they said, no extra pitch. It keeps the DMs honest and fast to send.

If you want a light personal touch, offer it for fit 5 only: one short clause that refers to what they actually wrote. Never make up a detail, and never flatter.

## Rules

- One message, one or two lines. No follow-up sequence.
- Never claim anything the offer page doesn't say.
- Never pretend to be a customer or a neutral third party.
- Drafts only. You open each profile and send them yourself.
