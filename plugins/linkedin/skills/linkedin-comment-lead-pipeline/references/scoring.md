# Scoring engagers 0 to 5

The score always comes from your own ICP, never from a fixed list of job titles. Build the rubric once per run, show it in one short block, and use it for every person.

## Build the rubric from your ICP

From the ICP page (GTM Context Kit) or your profile file, pull:
- **Hard filters:** what must be true (for example role, company type, size band, geography).
- **Public signals:** what makes someone a stronger fit and can be seen on LinkedIn or in company data (for example "runs a team", "company is hiring sales", "uses a tool you integrate with").
- **Anti-ICP:** who is never a fit.
- **Competitors:** who sells what you sell, or teaches it.
- **Best customers:** the roles and company types that look like your best customers, if listed.

If you have more than one ICP, score against the closest one and name it in the reason ("fits ICP 2").

## The scale

- **5: Bullseye.** Meets every hard filter and shows 2 or more public signals. Looks like your best customers.
- **4: Strong.** Meets the hard filters. Some doubt on seniority, budget or company size.
- **3: Moderate.** Right kind of role, but a hard filter is unclear or slightly off. Or an adjacent role that sometimes buys.
- **2: Weak.** Sells or teaches what you sell (a competitor), very junior, or a thin fit.
- **1: Poor.** Clearly in your anti-ICP.
- **0: Not a prospect.** A company page, the post author, or your own team.

Example rubric (invented, for a business that sells a client-onboarding tool to small agencies):

```
5: founder or ops lead at a 5 to 50 person agency, hiring or growing
4: founder or ops lead at an agency, size unclear
3: account manager at an agency, or founder of a consultancy
2: sells onboarding or automation services to agencies (competitor)
1: in-house marketer at a large enterprise (anti-ICP)
0: company page, author, own team
```

## Scoring tips

- **Headline first, on the first pass.** The headline is how someone positions themselves. On the final pass, an enrichment match by LinkedIn URL is usually the better source for the current employer, because headlines go stale. A match by name only can be the wrong person: then trust the headline.
- **Competitors are capped at 2**, even when the title says founder or CEO. Someone whose headline openly sells what you sell is a peer, not a buyer.
- **Founders who use your category of tool to run a different business** are not competitors. Score them on fit.
- **The comment counts.** A comment that describes the exact problem you solve can lift a 3 to a 4. A generic "Great post!" adds nothing.
- **Engagement depth breaks ties, it doesn't set the score.** Within the same score: a commenter ranks above a reactor, and someone who engaged with several posts ranks above someone who engaged with one.
- **A usable email breaks the next tie.** The script sorts by fit, then email, then engagement.
- **Reactors have no comment to read.** Their first score rests on the headline alone. Be stricter: when in doubt, give a 3, not a 4.
- **Write a specific reason.** "Ops lead at a 30-person agency, hiring 2 account managers" beats "good fit".
