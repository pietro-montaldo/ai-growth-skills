# Email structure

## Shape

1. **Subject:** short and specific. "Recap and next steps: onboarding for your sales team." If a thread exists, reply into it with `Re:` instead.
2. **Opener:** one line that refers to the call. "Thanks for the call today. Here's the recap." No filler.
3. **What we discussed:** bullets.
4. **As promised:** bullets. Drop the section if nothing was promised.
5. **Next steps:** two short sub-lists, **On my side** and **On your side**.
6. **Sign-off:** short, in your voice. "Any questions, just reply." then your name.

Use these exact section headers, bold, so every prospect sees the same structure.

## What each section holds

**What we discussed.** The key points, one or two lines each. Their situation and goals, team size and who else is involved, the options or approach you talked through, format and timing, anything that shapes price. A recap, not minutes. Three to six bullets.

**As promised.** Only the things you committed to send: a link, a file, a recording, a proposal, a case study, an intro. Each bullet has the real asset (link or "attached"). Never a placeholder. Never advice or topics: those belong above.

**Next steps, on my side.** Only what you committed to on the call, with a date if one was said.

**Next steps, on your side.** What they need to decide or do: choices to make, numbers to confirm, approvals, people to bring in, dates. Each with a date if one was said. If there's nothing: "Nothing pending from your side for now."

If a date wasn't set, write it as a proposal: "Shall we speak again in the week of the 14th?" Never invent certainty.

## Voice rules

- Use your voice skill or the samples you gave. Match the formality of the call: a founder intro reads looser than a procurement call.
- Short sentences. Plain and direct.
- No em dashes or double dashes. Use a period, comma or colon.
- Write ranges with "to": "15 to 20 people".
- No filler: "I hope this finds you well", "It was a pleasure", "I wanted to reach out", "Looking forward to our continued partnership".
- Clean, direct links. No tracking or redirect wrappers.
- If the company name never came up on the call and isn't in the attendee's email domain, don't guess it.

## HTML version

When creating an email draft, also give an HTML body: section headers in `<strong>`, bullets as `<ul><li>`. Nothing else fancy.

## Example (made-up call)

Subject: Recap and next steps: onboarding for your sales team

> Hi Dana,
>
> Thanks for the call today. Here's the recap.
>
> **What we discussed**
> - Your 12-person sales team is moving to a new CRM in March and wants the switch done without losing pipeline data.
> - Two options: a guided setup over 3 weeks, or a full done-for-you migration.
> - Sam from RevOps will own it on your side. Your COO signs off on anything over budget.
>
> **As promised**
> - The migration checklist: [link]
> - A short case study from a team of similar size: attached
>
> **Next steps**
>
> On my side:
> - Send a written proposal with both options by Friday.
>
> On your side:
> - Confirm which option you lean toward and the target go-live date.
> - Share the number of CRM records, roughly, so I can size the migration.
>
> Any questions, just reply.
>
> Alex

In a real draft, `[link]` is replaced with the actual URL before the draft is created.
