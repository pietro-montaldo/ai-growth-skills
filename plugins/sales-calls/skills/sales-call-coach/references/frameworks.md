# Frameworks

Pick one framework per sales call, from the call type in `call-classifier.md`. It is a second, structured look on top of the call basics, not a replacement.

## Which one

| Call type | Framework | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `discovery` | SPIN | Built for asking the right questions in the first real conversation |
| `intro_inbound` | Sandler pain funnel | Inbound prospects often arrive with a vague problem. Sandler digs in without scaring them off |
| `scoping` | MEDDIC | Several people, budget and process. Its pillars map to the deal |
| `demo_or_pitch` | Challenger | A demo without an insight is a feature tour. Checks whether you taught them something |
| `proposal_or_close` | MEDDIC + mutual action plan | Late stage. MEDDIC checks the deal is real, the plan maps the path to signature |
| `objection_or_negotiation` | Sandler upfront contract | Forces the question of what would actually unblock the deal |
| `follow_up` | Health check | Three questions only |

If a call straddles two types, pick the simpler framework. Forcing MEDDIC on a 12 minute intro is the failure to avoid.

## SPIN (discovery)

Four question types, in order. For each, mark whether you asked one, with a quote.

- **Situation:** facts about their current setup. Light touch, don't over-interview.
- **Problem:** what is broken or hard today.
- **Implication:** what that problem costs them in time, money, people or missed chances.
- **Need payoff:** what would change if the problem went away. Lets them sell themselves.

Coaching question: did you stop at Problem, or get to Implication? Most people stop at Problem. Implication is where the deal value is built.

## Sandler pain funnel (intro_inbound, objection_or_negotiation)

Layers that take a surface complaint to the real motive:

1. Tell me more about that.
2. Can you give me an example?
3. How long has that been going on?
4. What have you tried to fix it?
5. What does it cost you, in time, money or peace of mind?

Coaching question: how many layers did you reach before switching to your offer?

For objection calls, also check the upfront contract: did you agree at the start what the call would decide, and did you ask "what would need to be true for you to go ahead?"

## MEDDIC (scoping, proposal_or_close)

Six pillars. For each, a 1 to 5 score, one quote with its time, and the one most useful question you could have asked and didn't.

- **Metrics:** the number they care about (revenue, hours saved, cost avoided)
- **Economic buyer:** who can sign, and have you spoken with them
- **Decision criteria:** what they'll judge against (you vs a competitor, building it themselves, doing nothing)
- **Decision process:** who signs off, in what order, by when
- **Identify pain:** the named pain driving the purchase
- **Champion:** who on their side will push for this when you're not in the room

Scores:

- 1: not covered
- 2: mentioned, no detail
- 3: discussed, with gaps
- 4: clear answer on the call
- 5: clear answer with names, numbers and dates

Any pillar at 1 or 2 puts the deal at risk, however warm the call felt.

## Challenger (demo_or_pitch)

Three checks, one line each with a quote:

- **Teach:** did you share an insight they didn't already have about their problem?
- **Tailor:** did the demo speak to their situation, or was it a generic walkthrough?
- **Take control:** did you hold a recommendation, or let them steer into a feature tour?

## Mutual action plan (proposal_or_close, with MEDDIC)

Did you and the buyer agree a written list of:

- the steps from now to signature
- who owns each step
- a date for each step

Yes: the deal is in a real close cycle. No: it is hope. Coaching note: propose the plan on the next call.

## Health check (follow_up)

1. Is the problem still alive? (yes, softening, dead)
2. Anything new since the last call? (new person, new constraint, new budget)
3. Is the next step in a calendar? (yes, no)

Two or more "no" or "softening" answers: the deal is going cold.

## Output

In the review, render under **Framework: [name]**. One sentence on why it fits, then the output above. Keep it tight: the framework block is never longer than the call basics block.
