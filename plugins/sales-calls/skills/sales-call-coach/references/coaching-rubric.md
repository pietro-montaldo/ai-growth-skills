# Call basics rubric

Run on every sales call, whatever the type or framework. These are the basics any good sales coach watches. Most of what improves a close rate lives here, not in the framework.

For each item, give a one-line read with evidence. Skip an item only if it truly doesn't apply.

## Talk to listen ratio

Estimate your share of speaking time against theirs. With timestamps, use them. Without, estimate by word count and say it's an estimate.

Targets by call type:

- `discovery`, `intro_inbound`: you at 30 to 40 percent. Over 50 is the headline coaching note.
- `scoping`: 40 to 50 percent.
- `demo_or_pitch`: 50 to 65 percent. Over 80 means you never paused for a reaction.
- `proposal_or_close`: 30 to 50 percent. The buyer needs to talk through their decision out loud.
- `follow_up`: only matters if one side dominated.

Give the rough percentage and your longest single stretch.

## Longest monologue

Over 90 seconds in a discovery call is a flag. Over 3 minutes in any call is a flag. Give the topic and the time range.

## Question quality

Count your open and leading questions.

- Open: "what's making you look at this now", "walk me through how your team handles X today"
- Leading: "so you'd agree that...", "wouldn't it help if..."

Open questions surface real information. Leading questions confirm what you already assumed. In discovery, aim for mostly open.

## Buying signals caught or missed

A buying signal is when they say something that means "I'm closer to buying than a minute ago". For example:

- "How fast can you start?"
- "Would this work for the whole team?"
- "What does the contract look like?"
- "We tried X and it didn't work."
- "Our CEO has been pushing us on this."

Mark each signal:

- Caught: you reacted, dug deeper, moved the conversation forward
- Caught but rushed: you noticed and moved on too fast
- Missed: you kept talking past it

Missed signals are the single biggest thing to fix.

## Pain before pitch

In discovery, intro and scoping calls, you need to understand the pain before you pitch. Check whether you started describing your offer before they told you what hurts. If yes, give the time.

## Next step clarity

Every sales call should end with a specific next step: who, what, when.

- Specific: "I'll send the proposal Tuesday, you'll review it with your COO by Friday, we talk Monday at 10."
- Vague: "let me think about it", "we'll circle back"
- None: the call ended without a next step

Vague or none is a flag.

## Energy match

Did you match their pace and tone? A high-energy founder who got a flat reply is a mismatch. A reflective senior buyer hit with rapid-fire questions is a mismatch. Only mention this when there's a clear mismatch.

## Time on price vs time on value

If price came up: how long did you spend on price compared with the outcome they'd get? More time on price than value is a flag.

## Output

In the review, render this as a short bulleted block under **Call basics**, one line per item. If a metric is fine, say it in a few words ("ratio fine, 38 percent you"). Don't drop it silently.
