# Call extraction

Read the full transcript. The summary loses the numbers, the exact words and the hedges, and those are what a proposal is built on. Note the time of every quote you will rely on.

## What to pull

**People and decision**
- Who is the champion (wants it, will push for it) and who approves (signs, owns budget)?
- When do they decide, and what happens before that (a board meeting, a budget cycle, a leadership review)? Your delivery deadline is usually a little before that date.
- Who else was named and might need convincing?

**Scope**
- What they want done, in their words.
- Size: headcount, number of teams, sites, products, markets, volume. Note the number they want you to price on, which can differ from the total ("we're 40, but start with the 12 in sales").
- Team structure, and what makes splitting the work by team possible or impossible.
- What they already have and already paid for (tools, past vendors, internal attempts). Don't argue against a past purchase. Show how you build on it or remove its risk.
- Scope they declined, and anything you offered that they didn't take up.

**Outcomes**
- Pains they named without being asked, in their words. These go in the proposal almost verbatim.
- What success looks like for them: a number, a date, a capability, a feeling ("everyone at the same level").
- How they organise the work internally, if they told you. A structure they already use (one lead per team, a pilot group) can become the shape of your best option.

**Money and commitments**
- Every number said out loud, by either side: price, rate, budget, headcount, dates, hours. The client can replay the recording, so these are binding.
- Budget signals: a stated budget, a range, a comparison ("the last agency charged X"), hesitation at a number, "that's within what we expected".
- Every promise you made: what you would send, by when, at what price.

**Constraints and risks**
- Geography, time zones, languages, delivery window, blackout dates.
- Tools they use and anything sensitive (data that can't be shared, approval needed from IT or legal).
- Objections, and who raised them.
- Anything you owe them already (a recording, a link, an intro).

**Personalisation**
- Things you can use to tailor the proposal: their examples, their terms, a project they mentioned.

## Output

Put this in the working structure doc as short bullets under these headings. Mark anything you inferred as inferred. Flag anything garbled or uncertain in the transcript so you can confirm it.
