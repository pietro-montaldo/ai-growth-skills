# Pricing ladder

Prices come from your context or from you. This file is about checking them, not inventing them. When you ask for a price between two anchors, propose it in the structure doc, clearly marked as a proposal, and let you correct it.

## The ladder must read as fair value

Buyers compare options per unit, even when they don't say so. Before showing the table, compute for every option and at every quantity mentioned on the call:

- price per person (or per seat, per team, per unit, whatever the work is sized on)
- price per hour of delivery (or per day, per deliverable)
- price per prepared session or prepared deliverable, if preparation drives your cost

Flag:

- any option where the buyer pays **more per unit** than in the option below it, without a clear reason (more tailoring is a reason; more of the same is not)
- any step up with **zero discount** per unit
- any jump that is much bigger than the extra value it adds

Bigger options should read as a volume discount on the same quality, or as clearly more value (tailoring, support, speed).

### Example (made-up numbers)

| Option | Hours | People | Price | Per hour | Per person |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Core | 6 | 20 | 6,000 | 1,000 | 300 |
| Full | 12 | 20 | 10,800 | 900 | 540 |
| Full, tailored | 12 | 20 | 14,400 | 1,200 | 720 |

Reads fine: Full is cheaper per hour than Core, and the tailored option costs more per hour because of the preparation. If Full were 13,200, it would cost more per hour than Core with no extra tailoring. Flag it.

## Spoken numbers

Every number said on the call is binding, because the client can replay the recording. For each one:

- it matches the proposal exactly, or
- the email has one explicit line explaining the change ("The 8,000 we discussed was for 10 people. For 20 it's 10,800.")

Never change a spoken number silently.

## Second currency

If you show a second currency:

- Use the real exchange rate on the day, checked on two sources.
- Round up to a clean figure (for example the nearest 500 for five-figure prices, the nearest 50 for small ones).
- Footnote it: "Converted at the rate on [date], rounded up."
- Check small amounts: rounding up can add more than 25 percent to a small price. Flag those and round to a smaller step.

## Comparisons to a public price

If you compare the private price to a public one (your own public program, list price or per-seat rate):

- Check it per unit at the printed cap, against the live public price.
- Use "up to X% less per person", with X the best true figure, rather than a blanket claim that is false for half the table.
- Put the comparison in the email only, never in the proposal document.

## In the structure doc

Show the pricing table with the per-unit columns, the flags, and the spoken numbers with their times. Ask you to confirm or correct each flag.
