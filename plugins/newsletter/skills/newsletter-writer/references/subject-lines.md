# Subject lines and preview line

The subject line gets the email noticed. The preview line gives the reason to open. Together they decide how many readers see the edition at all, so think about them as carefully as the body.

## Subject lines: always 5

Write 5 options that use different approaches, not 5 small variations of one idea. Give each a one-line note on why it could work. End with a recommendation.

### Approaches

Pick the 5 that fit this edition.

**A. Relationship.** Signals trust, not a topic. Stands out in an inbox full of "number + topic" lines.
Pattern: "What I made for you this week" / "For subscribers only: [tease]"

**B. One outcome + a second hook.** A specific, tangible thing. Low numbers often beat high ones ("1 template" over "12 templates"). Add a second item after a "+".
Pattern: "1 [specific resource] + [second hook]"

**C. Curiosity.** Opens a loop the reader closes by opening the email. The email must actually close it.
Pattern: "The [thing] that changed how I [outcome]"

**D. Contrarian.** Breaks the pattern. Only when you really hold the view.
Pattern: "[Popular thing] is overrated. Here's what I use instead"

**E. Proof.** Leads with a real result, count or finding.
Pattern: "120 founders answered: [finding]" / "[Result] in [timeframe]"

**F. Question.** Something the reader really wants answered.
Pattern: "Still doing [outdated thing] by hand?"

**G. Timing.** Only when the urgency is real.
Pattern: "[Event] is Thursday: how to prepare"

**H. Complete guide.** When the main piece is large and structured (many chapters, sections or templates), lead with how complete it is. It signals "this is the one", not "another tip". Pairs well with naming the audience.
Pattern: "[Topic]: my full guide for [audience]" / "[N]-chapter [topic] playbook for [audience]"

### Rules

- Under about 50 characters where you can. Phones cut the rest.
- Named tools or platforms your readers know signal relevance.
- "Free" and "new" help. Don't overuse them.
- No more than 2 items. Never "A + B + C".
- Naming the audience ("for agency owners", "for first-time founders") helps when paired with build or guide language. Paired with explainer language it tends to hurt.
- "X vs Y" only works when readers know both names. If one is unfamiliar, drop the comparison.
- "What you need to know" and other explainer phrasing usually loses to "I built this", "my full guide" or "[N]-chapter" framing.
- Must sound like you. No em dashes or double dashes.
- For a teaching edition with a big structured resource, include at least one H option.

## Preview line: one, matched to the recommended subject

The preview line is the grey text shown next to or under the subject in the inbox. On some platforms it's a separate field; on others the subtitle or the first line of the email does the job (see `platform-formatting.md`).

- **Add, don't repeat.** Give new information: chapter count, time to use, what's included, the event date.
- **Subject + preview = one thought.** Read together they should make a complete case to open.
- **Front-load it.** The first 40 characters are the ones you can count on being seen. Aim for 40 to 90 in total.
- **Scope works well:** "6 chapters, short videos, step-by-step setup".
- Fragments are fine. No em dashes or double dashes.

If you pick a different subject line, rewrite the preview line to match it.

## Output format

Show the draft in this order:

```
SUBJECT LINE OPTIONS

1. "Client onboarding: my full guide for agency owners"
   Note: complete guide (H). Names the audience, signals a finished resource.
2. "1 onboarding template + the email clients reply to"
   Note: one outcome + second hook (B). Concrete and small.
3. "What I made for you this week"
   Note: relationship (A). Stands out from topic-led lines.
4. "Onboarding emails are overrated. Do this instead"
   Note: contrarian (D). Only if you really think so.
5. "Still onboarding clients by hand?"
   Note: question (F). Speaks to a pain they feel.

Recommendation: 1, because the main piece is a structured guide.

PREVIEW LINE (pairs with 1)
5 emails, ready to copy. 20 minutes to set up.

DRAFT
[the full edition in markdown, with formatting markers]

ASSUMPTIONS
- [one line per assumption]
```

The examples above are made up. Write yours from this edition's material.
