# Teaching, not selling

A newsletter is not a social post. Social posts compete for the scroll, so they lean on hooks, bold claims and cliffhangers. Newsletter readers already opened your email because they trust you. They want to learn something. Write like a teacher, not a marketer, and the offer (when there is one) lands better because the rest of the email earned it.

The user's own voice always comes first. These rules sit on top of it: they remove the selling energy, not the personality.

## Tone rules

- **State, don't sell.** "A template handles one task. A system handles the whole workflow." is the right register. "Most people hit a ceiling" or "This is NOT another guide" is too promotional.
- **Teach the idea, then the takeaway.** Explain it cleanly, then say what to do with it. Don't prop it up with hype.
- **Personal is fine. Flexing is not.** "I use this every week for client reports" is good. "This is the system behind my entire business" is too much.
- **Numbers over adjectives.** "120 people signed up" beats "a huge turnout". "Saves about 3 hours a week" beats "saves tons of time". Only real numbers you were given.
- **Use contrasts to teach choices.** "Use X when... Use Y when..."
- **Cut lines that don't teach.** If a sentence only says how good, unique or important the content is, delete it.
- **Don't hedge.** Drop "might", "perhaps", "could potentially" when you mean it.
- **Trust the reader.** Don't over-explain. They can click.

## Sentence shapes to avoid

These manufacture interest instead of delivering it. Replace them with a flat statement of the fact, or delete them and let the next sentence carry the section.

- "THIS is not a [category]..." openings
- "Here's why..." and "That is why..." bridges
- "X hit a ceiling" and "X is the reason Y fails"
- "This is the part most people miss." / "This is where most people get stuck." / "This is where it gets interesting." / "Here's what nobody tells you." / "And this is the kicker." / "The truth is..."
- Any "This is [the X / where Y]" line that promises insight without giving it in the same sentence
- "These aren't just demos" and "This is the setup behind..." flex lines
- Repeating "free", "all yours", "I'm giving it away" in the body. Once, in the "what's inside" list, is enough.
- Machine-gun calls to action: "Import. Edit. Run."
- "If you missed this on LinkedIn..." or anything that says the content was shared elsewhere first
- Warm-up openers: "In this edition, we're excited to share..."

Also avoid corporate words ("leverage", "synergy", "unlock your potential"), plus anything on your own avoid list.

## Turning material into sections

**A social post into the main piece**
1. Cut about half. Keep only the essentials.
2. Remove the hooks, cliffhangers and "comment X to get it" lines.
3. Remove hype words ("game-changing", "insane", "revolutionary").
4. Keep results and credentials: those are proof, not hype.
5. Find the one core insight and lead with it.
6. Rebuild it as: why it matters, what's inside, how to get it.

**A guide or template into the main piece**
Cover: the problem it solves, why it's credible (the story), how it works in a few short steps, a selective tease of what's inside, and the button. The story takes more space than the list. Never list every item.

**A video into the video section**
One or two sentences that say what it shows or what view it takes. Then the embed.

**An event into the event section**
Title, what people leave with, the real date and time, cost, guest and credential if any, button.

## Where the offer goes

Only when you said this edition promotes something. Otherwise leave it out completely.

- **One offer block per edition**, with its own heading. Don't sprinkle mentions through the body.
- **Teaching edition:** put it after the second or third idea subheading. Readers have already got value, and it gets seen. At the end, most never reach it.
- **Roundup edition:** put it near the end, after the main piece and events.
- **Shape:** one line on who it's for and what they get, one line on why now (a real start date, deadline or limited spots), the button. Frame anything exclusive as a thank-you to subscribers, not pressure.
- **The main link still wins.** The offer is a secondary click unless the whole edition is about the offer. If it is, say so in step 2 and treat the offer page as the main link.
- **Use your words for the offer** from your GTM context or profile, not new claims. No invented discounts, deadlines or results.

## Final checks

Run these before handing back the polished version:

1. **Voice:** does it sound like your samples? Same sentence length, same openings, same sign-off?
2. **Opening:** edition number or title, then the "what's inside" list, no warm-up?
3. **One main link:** can you name the link that carries the edition? Does every section point back to it or stand clearly apart?
4. **Tease, don't dump:** is the email shorter than the resource? Is the full contents list hidden?
5. **Structure:** does the "what's inside" list match the sections? Is the offer block (if any) where it should be? Dividers in a roundup?
6. **Shapes:** none of the sentence shapes above? No em dashes or double dashes?
7. **Facts:** every date, time, link, number, guest credential and "next time" topic came from you or your context?
8. **Preview line:** still matches the chosen subject line?
