# Edition types

Every edition is one of two types. Both share the opening, one main link, the optional sections and the sign-off. They differ in how much the email itself teaches, and where an offer block sits.

If you already have a structure your readers know, keep it and use these as a guide, not a replacement.

## Teaching edition

The email itself explains the core ideas of one main piece. Several short subheadings each teach one idea. The main link is "the full version" for readers who want to go deeper.

**Use it when:** there is one deep resource (a guide, a course module, a long video, a framework) worth explaining.

### Skeleton

```
0. Edition number or title
1. What's inside (short list)
2. Main piece title + framing (1 to 3 short paragraphs)
3. Early link line: "I'll walk you through the key ideas here, or you can open the full guide now:" + button
4. Idea subheading 1
5. Idea subheading 2
6. Offer block, if this edition promotes (here, after 2 or 3 ideas, not at the end)
7. Idea subheading 3 (and 4 to 6 if needed)
8. "What's inside the full version" (short tease) + button
9. Events, if any
10. Next time (1 or 2 topics), if any
11. Sign-off
```

### Rules

- 3 to 6 idea subheadings. Each teaches one idea, cleanly and completely.
- Short lists of 3 to 5 items work well for steps and options.
- "Use X when... Use Y when..." contrasts teach a decision without pushing an opinion.
- One analogy is fine if it really clarifies. Map each part of it.
- In a long edition, repeat the main button once in the middle.
- Save the full chapter or section list for the end, and tease it. Don't open with it.

## Roundup edition

A short, scannable index. Each section teases one resource and sends the reader to its link. No deep teaching inside the email.

**Use it when:** there are several resources, or the main piece is a template or collection rather than a tutorial.

### Skeleton

```
0. Edition number or title
1. What's inside (short list)
   [divider]
2. Main piece (story + tease + button)
   [divider]
3. Events, if any
   [divider]
4. Video, if any
   [divider]
5. Offer block, if this edition promotes (here, near the end)
   [divider]
6. Bonus, if any (a poll result, a study, one extra tool)
   [divider]
7. Next time (up to 3 topics), if any
   [divider]
8. Sign-off
```

### Rules

- 4 or 5 sections is plenty. Cut the weakest item before adding a sixth.
- A divider between every section makes it easy to scan.
- The main piece still gets the most space, even in a roundup.

## Section guide

### Opening

Start with the edition number or title on its own line, then the "what's inside" list. No warm-up sentence before it. Readers already opened; give them the map.

```
**Edition 14**

This week:
**Guide:** Client onboarding in 5 emails
**Live workshop:** Pricing your first retainer
**Bonus:** What 120 readers said about follow-ups
```

Use the labels from your past editions if you have them.

### Main piece (teaching edition)

```
### [Main piece title]

[Why it matters, 2 to 4 sentences. Who it's for, what problem it solves.]

[Proof, if you have it: what it did for you or others, with the real number.]

[Scope line: "6 chapters. About 40 minutes."]

I'll walk you through the key ideas here, or you can open the full guide now:

[Button: "Open the full guide"] → [link]

### [Idea 1]
...
```

### Main piece (roundup edition)

The pattern that gets clicks: story first, then a selective tease, then the button. Readers should want to see the rest before they reach the link.

```
### [Main piece title]

[Story, 2 to 4 sentences: why you made it, what it did, why it matters now.]

**What's inside:**
- [Highlight 1]
- [Highlight 2]
- [Highlight 3]
- [and 12 more templates]

[Ease line: "15 minutes to set up. No code."]

[Image: a cropped preview, not the whole contents]

[Button: "Get the templates"] → [link]
```

- The story takes more space than the list.
- Tease 3 to 5 highlights. If there are 20 items, show 5 and say how many more.
- Never show the full contents, in text or in a screenshot. When readers see everything, they feel they already have it and don't click.

### Ease lines

Every main piece gets at least one line that tells the reader how easy it is to use:
- Setup time: "15 minutes to set up."
- Ready to use: "Copy the template and change three fields."
- What the result looks like: "Here's an example output."
- Scope: "6 chapters. About 40 minutes."

### Events

```
### Live workshop: [Title]

[1 or 2 sentences: what people will build or leave with.]

**When:** Thursday, 12 March, 16:00 UTC
**Cost:** Free

[Button: "Save a seat"] → [link]
```

With a guest, put them up front:

```
I'm hosting this one with **[Guest name]**, **[role] at [company]**. [One credential line they approved.]
```

- Use the real date and time from the event page.
- Use the event's actual title. Not "my presentation".
- Several events can share one heading, such as "Live sessions this month".

### Video

One or two sentences on what the video shows or what view it takes, then the embed marker.

### Bonus

Short. One idea, one link at most.

### Next time

A short list of what's coming. Teaching editions: 1 or 2 topics. Roundups: up to 3. Only topics you gave.

### Sign-off

Your own sign-off, from your past editions. If you have none, a short line inviting replies works: readers replying is good for deliverability and gives you material for the next edition.
