# ICP and the 0 to 5 score

This skill has no ICP of its own. It scores against yours. This file says how to turn your ICP into a scoring card, how to tell when it's too vague, and how to turn a lead's research into a number.

## Build the scoring card

The card has four parts. Show it to the user before any research starts.

| Part | What goes in it | Example (made up) |
|---|---|---|
| **Must-haves** | Things that must all be true. Checkable from public data | Head of Sales or founder; B2B services firm; 10 to 200 people; UK or Ireland |
| **Strong signals** | Things that make a lead better. Each says where to check it | Hiring sales reps (job posts); posts about pipeline on LinkedIn; raised money in the last year |
| **Negatives** | Things that make a lead worse but don't rule them out | Very new in role; company shrinking |
| **Out** | Anti-ICP. Who looks like a fit but isn't | Sells sales training themselves (competitor); student; job seeker |

**From GTM context:** the ICP page already has this shape. Hard filters are the must-haves. Public scoring signals with `+2` or `+1` are strong signals, `-1` and `-2` are negatives. The anti-ICP is "Out". Copy them across, keep the "where to check" column, and keep the evidence tags (`(customers)`, `(guess, confirm)`) so the user sees which parts are unproven.

**From a profile file or answers:** draft the card from what the user said, mark every line you inferred `(guess, confirm)`, and ask them to fix it.

## The vague-ICP check

Run this before scoring. An ICP is too vague to score against when any of these is true:

- **No role.** "Businesses" or "companies that need X". Who in the company?
- **No size or type.** "Founders" covers a solo coach and a 500-person company. Which one?
- **A feeling, not a fact.** "Ambitious", "growth-minded", "would love this", "good companies". None of these can be checked before contact.
- **No "out" list.** If nobody is excluded, everybody scores 3.
- **Unclear logic.** A list of traits with no word on whether all must be true or any one is enough.

When it fails, ask the questions in SKILL.md step 2, then rewrite the card. If a trait still can't be checked from public data, move it to "check on the call" and leave it out of the score. Better to score on three clear facts than on eight fuzzy ones.

## The 0 to 5 scale

- **5, bullseye.** Meets every must-have and shows the strongest signals. Looks like the user's best customers.
- **4, strong.** Meets every must-have, with one point of doubt (seniority, size or budget).
- **3, moderate.** Meets most must-haves, unclear on one; or fits a secondary ICP; or the right role in an adjacent company type.
- **2, weak.** Misses a must-have, or matches a negative strongly, or sells what the user sells (competitor).
- **1, poor.** Clearly in "Out": wrong role, wrong layer, no path to buying.
- **0, not a prospect.** A company page, a duplicate, a test entry or an unusable row.

Bands: Strong is 4 or 5, Moderate is 3, Weak is 0 to 2.

If the card has points (from GTM public signals), use them inside a band: a lead who meets every must-have and has `+3` or more is a 5, one with `+1` or `+2` is a 4. A missed must-have caps the lead at 2, whatever the points say.

## Scoring tips

- The person's real current role and seniority is the main signal. A current profile or company page beats a stale title in the file.
- Anyone who clearly sells the same thing as the user is a competitor. Cap them at 2, even if the title says "founder".
- An owner of a business outside the user's category who would use what the user sells is a buyer, not a competitor.
- Break ties by whether a usable work email exists. Email-having leads rank higher within the same score. The script does this.
- With thin data (name only, private profile), score conservatively and say "provisional" in the rationale.
- When it's borderline, score up. The user makes the final call, and a maybe is cheaper to drop than a miss is to find.

## Next step lines

Concrete and short: the offer or angle to lead with and the hook from the research. "Lead with the audit offer. Hook: they posted last week about losing two reps." For 0 or 1, write "skip". Never invent an offer, price or result the user didn't give you.
